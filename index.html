This project focused on the aerodynamic assessment of a Formula 1-inspired race car using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). A 3D vehicle model was developed in CAD and prepared for external-flow simulation to investigate airflow characteristics, pressure distribution and aerodynamic force generation.

The study combined CAD modelling and numerical analysis to evaluate the relationship between vehicle geometry and aerodynamic performance, with emphasis on drag, downforce and flow-field characteristics.

02  |  CFD workflow

Methodology, set-up & results

The 3D vehicle geometry was prepared for external-flow simulation in SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation. The computational domain and flow conditions were defined to investigate the interaction between the incoming airflow and the vehicle's aerodynamic surfaces.

Reference model: Toyota TS010

A 3D model of the Toyota TS010 Group C prototype was developed as part of the thesis, providing a reference vehicle for comparative aerodynamic investigation. It represents a different racing-car configuration, enabling the examination of differences in body geometry, aerodynamic surfaces and overall vehicle proportions.

Simulation set-up

The simulation focused on the external airflow field around the vehicle, with particular attention to the computational domain, boundary conditions and mesh resolution.

Computational domain

Defined to enclose the vehicle and capture the external flow field, including upstream flow development, interaction with the bodywork and downstream wake formation.

Boundary conditions

Assigned to define the incoming airflow, outlet pressure and the interaction between the fluid and vehicle surfaces.

Mesh configuration

A computational mesh around the geometry represents the aerodynamic surfaces and resolves important flow features, including regions of potential separation and wake development.

Airflow simulation results

CFD was used to investigate airflow around the Formula 1 and Toyota TS010 models, comparing pressure distribution, flow velocity and wake development.

Comparative interpretation

The comparison examines how differences in vehicle geometry and aerodynamic configuration influence external airflow. Pressure distribution, velocity patterns and wake development provide insight into the aerodynamic characteristics of each design.
